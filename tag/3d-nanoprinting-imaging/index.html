3D printed designer color routers with low refractive index for low-light imaging

Nature Communications · 2026
DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-76072-8

Summary

Conventional color filters discard much of the incident light when selecting red, green and blue channels, limiting image quality under weak illumination. This study explores color routers that direct different wavelengths toward separate detection regions instead of relying primarily on absorption. The routers are inverse-designed and fabricated by two-photon polymerization using a low-refractive-index resin, with an index of approximately 1.5. Their three-dimensional geometry redistributes visible light at the pixel scale. Simulations predict about 87% transmittance, while experiments reach approximately 70%; these values represent different stages of validation and should not be treated as interchangeable. Electron microscopy and optical measurements characterize the printed structures, and imaging with a monochrome sensor demonstrates improved color fidelity and shorter exposure times. The results show that efficient color routing is possible with low-index printable materials, suggesting an alternative optical architecture for improving light utilization in future imaging systems.

Nanoscale 3D printing of glass photonic crystals with near-unity reflectance in the visible spectrum

Science Advances · 2025
DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.adv0267

Summary

Fabricating three-dimensional glass structures at visible-light length scales is difficult because both the printing resolution and the conversion process must preserve fine geometry. This work introduces Glass-Nano, an organic–inorganic hybrid resin containing silicon, for two-photon lithography. After printing, heating in air removes the organic components and converts the remaining structure into silica glass. Sintering at 650 °C produces three-dimensional photonic crystals with periodicities as small as 260 nm. The resulting crystals exhibit near-unity reflectance in the visible spectrum, despite glass having a comparatively low refractive index. Electron microscopy reveals well-defined nanoscale architecture, and measured optical responses agree closely with calculations for idealized photonic-crystal structures. The study links resin chemistry, precision printing and thermal conversion to achieve functional glass nanophotonics. It establishes a route to complex three-dimensional optical structures whose performance depends on nanoscale geometric control.

Single-Shot 3D Imaging Meta-Microscope

Nano Letters · 2024
DOI: 10.1021/acs.nanolett.4c03952

Summary

Three-dimensional microscopy often requires axial scanning or bulky optical components to determine an object’s depth. This work uses an all-dielectric metasurface to create a double-helix point-spread function, whose rotating two-lobe image encodes axial position. The concept is demonstrated in both 4f and 2f microscope configurations, including an integrated double-helix metalens. The 4f system, with a numerical aperture of 0.7, achieves axial localization accuracy below 0.12 µm over a 15.47 µm detection range. The 2f system, with a numerical aperture of 0.3, achieves 1.12 µm accuracy over a larger 227.33 µm range. Fabricated metasurfaces are characterized optically and by electron microscopy, and single-shot biological imaging is demonstrated using mouse kidney tissue and peach anther. The results illustrate how metasurface-based wavefront control can trade localization precision against depth range while simplifying acquisition of three-dimensional information.

Pick and place process for uniform shrinking of 3D printed micro- and nano-architected materials

Nature Communications · 2023
DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-41535-9

Summary

Thermal shrinking can improve the feature size of objects fabricated by two-photon polymerization, but attachment to the printing substrate can produce uneven shrinkage and distortion. This study introduces a poly(vinyl alcohol)-assisted pick-and-place process that transfers printed structures to a receiving substrate before heating. The transfer changes the mechanical boundary conditions and allows the structures to contract more uniformly without requiring sacrificial support architectures. Experiments compare receiving surfaces and show that their surface properties strongly influence the final shape. Microscopy confirms uniform shrinking across objects with different sizes and geometries. The process also produces vivid structural colors by shrinking initially colorless woodpile photonic crystals, including those incorporated into a three-dimensional mascot model. By separating the printing substrate from the shrinking substrate, the work provides a practical way to refine printed micro- and nano-architectures for optical, photonic and mechanical applications.
