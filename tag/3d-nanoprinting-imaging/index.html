3D printed designer color routers with low refractive index for low-light imaging

Nature Communications · 2026
DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-76072-8

Summary

Inverse-designed, three-dimensional color routers direct different wavelengths toward corresponding detector pixels instead of discarding light through conventional color filtering. The structures are printed by two-photon polymerization in a low-index resin. The study reports approximately 70% experimental transmission across the visible spectrum and demonstrates improved color fidelity with shorter exposures on a monochrome sensor, showing potential for more efficient low-light imaging.

Nanoscale 3D printing of glass photonic crystals with near-unity reflectance in the visible spectrum

Science Advances · 2025
DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.adv0267

Summary

A silicon-containing hybrid resin enables three-dimensional printing of glass photonic crystals with nanoscale features. Two-photon lithography forms the initial structures, and heat treatment removes organic components to create silica glass. The resulting crystals reach periodicities as small as 260 nanometers and nearly complete reflection at selected visible wavelengths. Electron microscopy and optical modeling support the structural quality and measured photonic response.

Single-Shot 3D Imaging Meta-Microscope

Nano Letters · 2024
DOI: 10.1021/acs.nanolett.4c03952

Summary

An all-dielectric metasurface creates a double-helix point-spread function that encodes depth in a single image. The work integrates this approach into two microscope configurations with different localization ranges and numerical apertures. Experiments demonstrate three-dimensional imaging of mouse kidney tissue and peach anther. This provides a compact route to depth-resolved microscopy without the scanning arrangements commonly used in conventional systems.

Pick and place process for uniform shrinking of 3D printed micro- and nano-architected materials

Nature Communications · 2023
DOI: 10.1038/s41467-023-41535-9

Summary

A poly(vinyl alcohol)-assisted pick-and-place process releases printed three-dimensional microstructures from their original substrate before heat treatment. This reduces substrate pinning and enables more uniform shrinkage of different shapes and sizes without sacrificial supports. The work also demonstrates visible structural colors from shrunken woodpile photonic crystals, establishing a practical route to finer features in printed optical and mechanical structures.
