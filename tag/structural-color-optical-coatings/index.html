Industrial-Grade Structural Coloration with Robust and Vivid TiON-Based Structures

Advanced Optical Materials · 2026
DOI: 10.1002/adom.202503135

Summary

Structural-color coatings must retain their appearance under practical environmental and mechanical stresses. Conventional metal–insulator–metal cavities can lose color quality when the exposed top metal oxidizes or wears. This work replaces that layer with titanium oxynitride (TiON), a hard, corrosion-resistant lossy dielectric, and combines it with a silicon nitride spacer and a metallic reflector. Changing the layer thicknesses produces vivid colors through resonant interference and absorption. The fabricated coatings are evaluated using reflectance measurements, camera imaging, color-coordinate analysis and electron microscopy, together with five-year aging, humidity and abrasion tests. The results show absorption exceeding 90% at the resonance and comparatively small spectral shifts of around 10 nm under the reported conditions. The study connects optical design with durability testing, demonstrating a planar structural-color platform intended for industrial coatings where long-term color retention matters as much as the initial appearance.

Widening the Gamut of Structural Colors of Gold via Insulator–Metal Bilayer Coatings

Advanced Photonics Research · 2024
DOI: 10.1002/adpr.202300324

Summary

Changing the appearance of gold usually involves alloying, which can require different compositions for different colors. This study instead investigates a lithography-free surface coating that tunes color through thin-film interference. A titanium dioxide layer deposited directly on gold offers a limited color range. Introducing a 50–100 nm AuAl2 underlayer substantially broadens that range, while varying the TiO2 thickness shifts the reflectance minimum and selects the reflected hue. The coating approach modifies the surface without requiring the underlying gold body to be alloyed throughout. Fabricated samples are assessed through reflectance spectroscopy, camera photographs, color-coordinate analysis and cross-sectional microscopy. Simulations reproduce the main experimental trends and explain the role of the optical properties of the AuAl2 layer. The results provide a simple thickness-based design strategy for decorative gold surfaces with a wider range of colors than a dielectric coating on gold alone.

In Situ Metal Deposition on Perhydropolysilazane-Derived Silica for Structural Color Surfaces with Antiviral Activity

ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces 15(46), 54143–54156 · 2023
DOI: 10.1021/acsami.3c12622

Summary

This work combines structural coloration with antimicrobial functionality using a solution-based metal–silica coating on stainless steel. Perhydropolysilazane converts into silica while releasing hydrogen, which reduces metallic species directly on the surface to form nanoparticles. The resulting metal–dielectric–metal arrangement produces color through Fabry–Pérot interference, and patterned deposition demonstrates decorative surfaces without a complex vacuum-processing sequence. The films also show water repellency, with contact angles reaching 137°. Silver-containing coatings are evaluated in separate laboratory antiviral and antibacterial assays. Lentivirus inhibition is reported as 75%, 97% and 99% under different assay methods and exposure conditions; these figures describe distinct tests, not a single universal efficacy value. The study also reports suppression of bacterial colony growth under its test conditions. Together, the results demonstrate decorative coatings with additional surface functionality and motivate further assessment of their durability and antimicrobial performance in practical environments.

Realization of Structural Colors via Capped Cu-based F–P Cavity Structure

Optics Express 29(18), 29466–29480 · 2021
DOI: 10.1364/OE.435768

Summary

Copper offers a lower-cost alternative to noble metals for structural-color coatings, but its exposed surface needs protection from oxidation and mechanical damage. This study uses a copper–silicon nitride–copper asymmetric Fabry–Pérot cavity to generate reflective colors. Varying the thickness of the top copper layer or the dielectric spacer shifts the optical response and produces hues including orange, red, purple, blue and green-yellow. Modeling and reflectance measurements attribute the colors to thin-film interference. The work then compares silica, PMMA and a commercial clear coat as protective layers, examining both the optical appearance and environmental response. PMMA preserves the color while protecting the copper under the reported corrosive conditions. Photographs at different viewing angles document the appearance of fabricated samples. The results show how inexpensive optical materials and transparent protection can be combined in a simple, lithography-free coating for decorative metal surfaces.

Structural Colors on Al Surface via Capped Cu-Si3N4 Bilayer Structure

Micromachines · 2023
DOI: 10.3390/mi14020471

Summary

This paper develops tunable structural colors on aluminum using a copper–silicon nitride optical cavity. The aluminum acts as a reflector, the silicon nitride provides the dielectric spacing, and a thin copper layer controls the partially reflecting top interface. Adjusting the copper or dielectric thickness generates a range of colors through thin-film interference. To make the exposed copper more practical, the study compares silica, ladder-like polysilsesquioxane, PMMA and a commercial clear coat as protective layers. The experiments examine how these coatings affect appearance, viewing-angle dependence and resistance to humidity or corrosion. Selected polymer coatings and polymer–silica bilayers preserve the color while improving protection under the tested conditions. Measurements of the copper dielectric response provide no evidence that a plasmonic resonance is responsible for the observed colors. The work therefore combines a simple interference design with surface-protection strategies for decorative aluminum coatings.

To realize a variety of structural color adjustments via lossy-dielectric-based Fabry–Perot cavity structure

Nanophotonics · 2022
DOI: 10.1515/nanoph-2022-0522

Summary

Planar Fabry–Pérot coatings can generate vivid colors without nanoscale patterning, but the thin top metal in conventional designs is vulnerable to oxidation and scratching. This study investigates chromium oxynitride (CrON) as a lossy dielectric replacement for that metal. CrON is combined with a silicon nitride spacer and a metallic back reflector, so that interference within the cavity and absorption in the top layer jointly determine the reflected color. Calculations identify thickness combinations capable of producing strongly saturated colors, and fabricated samples validate the main optical trends. The design also supports strong light absorption with top layers approaching 100 nm, providing more thickness freedom than a conventional thin metallic surface. Reflectance measurements and cross-sectional characterization connect the layer structure to the optical response. The results establish a lithography-free design route for color coatings and absorbers using an exposed lossy dielectric.

Reflection color tuning of a metal–insulator–metal cavity structure using arc plasma deposition of gold nanoparticles

Applied Surface Science 562, 150140 · 2021
DOI: 10.1016/j.apsusc.2021.150140

Summary

The optical behavior of a thin metal layer depends on whether it consists of isolated nanoparticles, a porous network or a continuous film. This study uses arc plasma deposition to control that progression in the top gold layer of an Au/Si3N4/Al Fabry–Pérot cavity. Increasing the number of deposition pulses changes the gold morphology and effective optical constants. Electron microscopy characterizes the evolving layer, while effective-medium modeling based on the Bruggeman approximation helps explain the reflectance. Combining different gold coverages with different silicon nitride thicknesses produces a broad range of structural colors. The dielectric thickness mainly controls hue, whereas increasing gold coverage generally increases chroma at a fixed spacer thickness. Patterned KIST logos demonstrate color printing on fabricated surfaces and its modest viewing-angle dependence. The work establishes morphology control as an additional design variable for interference coatings, alongside the more conventional adjustment of film thickness.

Polarizonic-interference colouration of stainless steel surfaces by Au-Al₂O₃ nanocomposite thin film coating

Applied Surface Science 505, 144428 · 2020
DOI: 10.1016/j.apsusc.2019.144428

Summary

This paper investigates structural coloration of stainless steel using gold–alumina nanocomposite thin films. In the as-deposited state, changing film thickness and composition produces colors including magenta, blue, yellow-green and orange. The optical response is interpreted through polarizonic interference, in which reflection associated with the disordered composite interacts with reflection from the underlying metal. Thermal annealing changes the microstructure: gold particles grow and the composite becomes more crystalline, bringing a stronger plasmonic contribution. The resulting color range narrows mainly to dark yellow-green and light orange. Electron microscopy, reflectance measurements and color analysis connect these changes in appearance to the evolving nanoscale structure. Effective-medium modeling using the Bruggeman approach is applied to the annealed samples. The study shows that both deposition conditions and subsequent heat treatment govern the available color palette, offering guidance for nanocomposite decorative coatings on stainless steel.
