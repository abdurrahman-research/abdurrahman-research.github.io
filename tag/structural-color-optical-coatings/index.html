In Situ Metal Deposition on Perhydropolysilazane-Derived Silica for Structural Color Surfaces with Antiviral Activity

ACS Applied Materials & Interfaces 15(46), 54143–54156 · 2023
DOI: 10.1021/acsami.3c12622

Summary

A low-cost, solution-based process creates structural colors using metal–silica multilayers. Hydrogen released as perhydropolysilazane converts to silica reduces metal nanoparticles directly on the surface. The resulting interference coatings combine vivid coloration with water repellency. Silver-coated samples also show antibacterial activity and inhibit lentivirus in laboratory assays, suggesting decorative surfaces with additional antimicrobial functionality.

Realization of Structural Colors via Capped Cu-based F–P Cavity Structure

Optics Express 29(18), 29466–29480 · 2021
DOI: 10.1364/OE.435768

Summary

Copper–silicon nitride–copper cavities produce vivid reflective colors through thin-film interference. Adjusting the copper or dielectric thickness tunes the appearance across multiple hues. The study compares transparent protective coatings, including silica, PMMA and a commercial clear coat. PMMA protects the copper structure in corrosive environments while preserving color quality, supporting decorative surfaces, optical filters and color-based sensing applications.

To realize a variety of structural color adjustments via lossy-dielectric-based Fabry–Perot cavity structure

Nanophotonics · 2022
DOI: 10.1515/nanoph-2022-0522

Summary

This study replaces the exposed top metal in a Fabry–Pérot color coating with a lossy chromium oxynitride dielectric. Modeling and experiments show that the resulting CrON/silicon-nitride/metal stacks generate vivid colors through interference and absorption. The design also supports strong light absorption with comparatively thick top layers, offering a planar approach to color control that addresses the vulnerability of exposed metallic surfaces.

Reflection color tuning of a metal–insulator–metal cavity structure using arc plasma deposition of gold nanoparticles

Applied Surface Science 562, 150140 · 2021
DOI: 10.1016/j.apsusc.2021.150140

Summary

Arc plasma deposition controls the gold layer in an Au/Si₃N₄/Al optical cavity. Increasing deposition pulses transforms dispersed nanoparticles into porous and then dense films, changing their optical properties. Interference produces a broad colour palette: dielectric thickness mainly sets hue, while gold coverage adjusts chroma. Effective-medium modelling helps explain the response of the discontinuous gold layers.

Polarizonic-interference colouration of stainless steel surfaces by Au-Al₂O₃ nanocomposite thin film coating

Applied Surface Science 505, 144428 · 2020
DOI: 10.1016/j.apsusc.2019.144428

Summary

Gold–alumina nanocomposite coatings give stainless steel tunable structural colours through polarizonic interference. Adjusting amorphous-film thickness and composition produces magenta, blue, yellow-green and orange. Annealing crystallizes the coating and enlarges its gold nanoparticles, restricting the palette to dark yellow-green and light orange as plasmonic effects become significant. Bruggeman modelling is used to predict the colours of the annealed samples.
