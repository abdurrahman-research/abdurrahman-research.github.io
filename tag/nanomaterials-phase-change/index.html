Tunable Multiresonant Microcavity Exciton-Polaritons in Colloidal Quantum Wells

Nano Letters · 2025
DOI: 10.1021/acs.nanolett.5c00015

Summary

Exciton–polaritons form when cavity photons couple strongly to electronic excitations, but controlling this interaction requires precise adjustment of the optical cavity length. This study develops a multiresonant microcavity array with cavity-length variations below 5 nm, providing a set of resonances across a single substrate. The platform incorporates gradient core–crown colloidal quantum wells and is used to investigate strong coupling at room temperature. Optical measurements reveal a Rabi-oscillation quality factor of 3.3 and a Rabi splitting exceeding twice the thermal losses. Changing the cavity resonance across the array controls the relative excitonic and photonic character of the polariton states. Fabrication, microscopy and spectroscopy connect the controlled cavity geometry to these optical properties. The work offers a chip-compatible route to selecting and studying different coupling conditions without mechanically adjusting an individual cavity, supporting future development of integrated polaritonic devices.

Continuously tunable optical states of highly textured Sb2Te3

Optical Materials Express · 2025
DOI: 10.1364/OME.564116

Summary

Phase-change photonics benefits from materials whose optical properties can be set to more than two discrete states. This paper investigates highly textured Sb2Te3 and its transition from metallic-like to dielectric behavior through intermediate levels of structural disorder. Thin films are prepared and characterized to relate crystallographic texture, heating and laser-induced changes to the measured optical response. Comparisons with Ge2Sb2Te5 highlight the more gradual evolution available in Sb2Te3. The measured material properties are then used in resonant thin-film designs and simulations that predict continuously adjustable reflected colors. These reflector designs are prospective optical structures; the study does not demonstrate a complete working display. By linking accessible intermediate material states with interference-based color control, the work identifies a route toward analog photonic tuning. Potential applications include nonvolatile reflective color elements that retain their optical state without a continuous holding power.

Controlling TiO2 photocatalytic behaviour via perhydropolysilazane-derived SiO2 ultrathin shell

Nanoscale · 2024
DOI: 10.1039/D4NR03566F

Summary

Titanium dioxide is valuable for its whiteness and high refractive index, but its photocatalytic activity can damage surrounding organic materials. This study suppresses that activity by forming an ultrathin silica shell around TiO2 particles using perhydropolysilazane (PHPS). The process uses TiO2-generated hydroxyl radicals to promote conversion of PHPS into silica, producing a self-limiting coating with comparatively uniform thickness. A conventional tetraethyl orthosilicate route is examined as a reference. Under the reported tests, PHPS-derived core–shell particles achieve 89–96% photocatalytic passivation, compared with about 30% for the reference particles. Coated thin films degrade only 12% of the Eosin B test dye, versus 80% for unprotected TiO2 films. Microscopy and optical measurements show that the protection preserves useful whiteness and refractive properties. The approach therefore combines surface passivation with retention of the optical characteristics needed for pigments and high-index coatings.

Deterministic positioning of few aqueous colloidal quantum dots

Nanoscale · 2024
DOI: 10.1039/D4NR02123A

Summary

Integrating colloidal quantum dots into photonic structures requires control over where small numbers of emitters are deposited. This work uses a lithographically patterned PMMA template to position aqueous, silica-coated quantum dots. During drop casting, a saturated ethanol-vapor environment changes the wetting and flow behavior of the solution, helping particles enter the patterned holes. A subsequent lift-off step removes particles outside the intended sites. Hole geometry controls the number of deposited particles, and the resulting occupancy follows a statistical distribution rather than guaranteed one-particle placement at every location. The study reports approximately 40% single-particle occupancy with an overall site occupancy around 80%. Electron microscopy and optical characterization assess the deposited arrays. This combination of template fabrication, vapor-assisted assembly and lift-off provides a practical route to placing a few emitters at predefined positions for subsequent integration with micro- and nanophotonic devices.
