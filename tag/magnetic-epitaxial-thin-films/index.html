On the growth and coalescence of the epitaxial islands of L10-FePt (001) thin-film

Journal of Applied Physics · 2016
DOI: 10.1063/1.4966169

Summary

The magnetic properties of epitaxial L10-FePt films depend strongly on how initially separate islands grow and merge. This study uses high-resolution transmission electron microscopy and Fourier analysis to investigate island formation and coalescence on MgO. Early growth produces discrete epitaxial islands, which develop into networks separated by narrow channels before becoming more continuous. The observations identify two coalescence pathways: a zipping-like closure and continued growth that fills the groove between neighboring islands. Detailed lattice-fringe analysis reveals boundary defects, including oppositely oriented tilt-boundary components that reduce interfacial strain. As coalescence progresses, the coercivity and magnetic nucleation field decrease, linking structural defects to changes in magnetic behavior. The work provides a microscopic account of how deposition and island interactions influence ordered magnetic thin films. Its relevance to magnetic recording lies in controlling the material microstructure, rather than demonstrating a completed recording device.

Effect of Li substitution on the magnetic properties of LixMg0.40Ni0.60−2xFe2+xO4 ferrites

Physica B: Condensed Matter · 2011
DOI: 10.1016/j.physb.2011.01.058

Summary

This study examines how lithium substitution and sintering conditions modify the structure and magnetic response of Li–Mg–Ni ferrites. Compositions in the series LixMg0.40Ni0.60−2xFe2+xO4 are investigated over x = 0–0.30. X-ray diffraction identifies the spinel structure, while microscopy and physical measurements show changes in lattice dimensions, density, porosity and grain size. Increasing lithium content generally increases the lattice constant and grain size. Initial permeability rises with lithium substitution up to approximately x = 0.25 and depends strongly on sintering temperature, with higher-temperature behavior varying by composition. Magnetization measurements show ferrimagnetic behavior at room temperature, and the trends are discussed in terms of cation distribution, microstructure and spin canting. The work demonstrates that composition and thermal processing jointly control the magnetic properties. It is a materials-characterization study, so the accompanying figures show diffraction and fabricated ceramic microstructures rather than an electronic device.
